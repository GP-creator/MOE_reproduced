# E1: why dense_x8 is not the upper bound (moe-architect diagnosis, 2026-10-05)

Draft text for the "What didn't replicate" sections of NOTES.md / RESULTS.md. Every number traces to `results/main/e1_main/*/{final.json,eval_log.jsonl,train_log.jsonl}`; re-check against the files when the final docs are generated.

DeepSeekMoE reports that its dense upper bound (same total parameters, all active) matches or beats every MoE. In our E1 main runs, dense_x8 (63.31M params, 129.0M fwd FLOPs/token) reached val loss 1.6785, behind DeepSeekMoE at 1.6602/1.6559 (seeds 1337/1338) and tied with GShard at 1.6783. dense_x8 had the lowest val loss of any run at step 150 (3.513), was worse than the 4.3×-cheaper dense baseline from step 600 to 3600 (by up to 0.054 at step 1200), and gained the most from the LR decay (0.232 from step 4650 to 6000, vs 0.203–0.218 for the other seven runs). We found no implementation issue: measured and analytic parameter counts match, init-time FFN output scale equals dense (std ≈ 0.041), and there were no loss spikes or skipped steps, with clipping confined to warmup. The likely cause is that the single peak LR (2e-3, chosen on the small config's 1024-wide dense and Switch FFNs) is too high for a 12288-wide FFN under standard parameterization: dense_x8's trained W_out moved 29× its init norm (dense 13×), and its FFN outputs and residual stream are about 2.4× larger than dense's. All 63M-parameter models also see only 0.78 tokens per total parameter (49.2M tokens), and dense_x8 has one seed, so this comparison is weak evidence about the paper's claim either way.
